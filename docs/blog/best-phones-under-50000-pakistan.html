Best Phones Under Rs 50,000 in Pakistan (2026) — Top 6 Picks

Published 2026-11-12 · Check Specs

Almost every week someone asks us the same question: "Fifty thousand mein kaunsa phone loon?" Fair question — and honestly, a harder one than it looks. This is the price range where shops push whatever gives them margin, where two phones with the same sticker price can feel completely different in the hand, and where one wrong variant (the 4 GB model instead of the 8 GB) can ruin an otherwise good purchase.

The six phones below are all on sale in Pakistan now for under Rs 50,000, and they are not interchangeable. Each suits a different kind of buyer, and the differences — screen technology, chipset, charging speed — matter more than the small gaps in price. Start here: Tecno Spark 30 Pro (Rs 41,499)

If you just want one recommendation, take this one and keep the change. It is the cheapest phone on this page, and the screen is the reason it leads the list. An AMOLED panel with a 120Hz refresh rate and a manufacturer-rated peak brightness of 1,700 nits is well above what this price usually buys. The main camera is a 108 MP unit, the Helio G100 is a capable chipset for everyday applications, and 33W charging is included. Unless gaming or maximum battery life is the priority, this is the default recommendation here. Full specs and price.

The gamer's phone: Infinix Hot 40 Pro (Rs 44,999)

PUBG and COD Mobile players, this one is aimed at you. The Helio G99 is the chipset budget gaming phones keep coming back to, for good reason, and Infinix pairs it with a 120Hz screen and up to 12 GB of RAM. On paper, that combination should deliver noticeably smoother gameplay than the G85 and G91-class chips elsewhere in this list. The 32 MP selfie camera has its own dual-LED flash, a genuinely useful addition for video calls in poor light. The battery is 5,000 mAh with 33W charging — Infinix advertises 20% to 75% in 35 minutes — which makes short top-ups between sessions practical. Full specs and price.

Camera first: Redmi 13 (Rs 44,999)

A useful distinction for phone cameras in this range: the megapixel count is largely marketing, but autofocus is not. The Redmi 13's 108 MP main camera has PDAF, so it locks focus quickly instead of hunting, which makes a practical difference with moving subjects. The 5,030 mAh battery is sized to cover a full day of typical use, and there is a 256 GB variant if you shoot a lot of photos and video. The 90Hz screen is a step behind the 120Hz crowd, and storage is the slower eMMC type, so large app installs and file transfers take longer than on UFS-equipped rivals. For camera-first buyers, it remains the strongest option here. Full specs and price.

Two days, no charger: Vivo Y28 (Rs 46,999)

The Y28 carries a 6,000 mAh battery, the largest in this list by a wide margin, and charges at 44W, so even that large battery refills quickly. Buyers who routinely spend long hours away from a charger, such as delivery riders, drivers and field staff, are exactly who this specification is aimed at. The trade-offs are real: the Helio G85 is a modest chipset, and the 720p screen is not sharp. The calculation will suit many working buyers: endurance and charging speed over display quality. Full specs and price.

The safe pair of hands: Samsung Galaxy A07 (Rs 47,999)

Some buyers walk into the shop already knowing they want a Samsung, and nothing written here will change their mind. Good news: they no longer have to settle for a weak phone to get the badge. The A07 runs the same Helio G99 as the Hot 40 Pro, has a 90Hz display, a 50 MP main camera with autofocus, and storage up to 256 GB with a dedicated memory card slot. Charging is a slower 25W and you pay a few thousand extra for the name — but you also get Samsung's service centres in every major city, which is worth something when a screen breaks in year two. Full specs and price.

Spend the whole budget: Infinix Note 50s 5G (Rs 49,999)

Right at the top of the budget sits the fastest phone in this list by a clear margin. The Dimensity 7300 Ultimate is a 4 nm chip — a different league from everything above — the AMOLED screen runs at 144Hz, and the 5,500 mAh battery is rated for a full charge in about 60 minutes at 45W. It is also the only 5G phone in this list. Pakistan's 5G rollout is still a promise rather than a reality, so do not buy it for 5G alone; buy it because its chipset has the most headroom, and is the most likely here to still feel responsive in three years. Full specs and price.

Before you pay — three things shops won't tell you

Check the variant. Several of these phones come in 4, 6 and 8 GB RAM versions that look identical. The price quoted to you is often for the smallest one. Ask, then check the box.

PTA approval matters. Buy the officially approved version with a proper warranty. A slightly cheaper non-PTA or CPID unit can stop working on local networks, and it is nearly impossible to resell later.

Prices move. The prices above are the Pakistan market prices we track on Check Specs and they do change — sometimes within the same week. Check the phone's page here on the day you buy; if a shop is asking well above it, you have your bargaining number.
