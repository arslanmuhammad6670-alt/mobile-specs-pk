Huawei Enjoy 100 Pro and Pro Max Price in Pakistan — One of Them Has an 8,800 mAh Battery

Published 2026-11-12 · Check Specs

Huawei does not launch phones in Pakistan the way it used to, so when two new models start showing up in search, people understandably want to know two things: what do they cost here, and is there a catch? With the Enjoy 100 Pro and Enjoy 100 Pro Max, the honest answers are "less than you would expect" and "yes, one — keep reading".

Both phones were announced on 28 October 2026, with release expected in early November. They are not officially on sale in Pakistan yet, so the prices below are expected prices based on the international figures — treat them as your bargaining compass, not a shop sticker.

The Pro Max is the interesting one

An 8,800 mAh silicon-carbon battery is, at the time of writing, about as big as phone batteries get — most flagships sit between 5,000 and 5,500 mAh. Huawei claims the phone is also drop resistant up to 1.8 metres and carries an IP65 rating, which makes the Pro Max feel like a phone built for people who are hard on phones: long days outdoors, travel, work sites. The screen peaks at a frankly absurd 8,000 nits on paper, so sunlight legibility should not be a problem.

There are trade-offs, and they are worth knowing before you fall for the battery number. The Pro Max is heavy at 228 g — you will feel it in a shirt pocket. Its selfie camera is only 8 MP, oddly worse than the cheaper Pro's 32 MP unit. And the Kirin 8020A chipset is a capable mid-range chip, not a flagship slayer.

The regular Pro is the sensible buy

The Enjoy 100 Pro keeps most of what matters: the same Kirin 8020A chip, a 6.7-inch OLED screen at 120Hz, a 50 MP main camera with autofocus, a better 32 MP selfie camera and a still-large 6,100 mAh battery with 40W charging. It is lighter (192 g), slimmer, and expected to cost about Rs 8,000 less. Unless you specifically need the giant battery, this is the one to watch.

The catch — read this before importing one

Both phones run HarmonyOS 7.0. That means no Google Play Store and no built-in Google apps; Huawei's own AppGallery and workarounds cover many popular apps, but banking apps, some Google services and a few local apps can be hit or miss. For a phone you plan to buy through unofficial channels in Pakistan, that software question matters more than any spec on this page. If your life runs on Google apps, think carefully. If it does not — and the battery tempts you — the Pro Max in particular is a genuinely unusual phone.
